The Big Idea

Lunch Beyond the Lunch Box.

A lunch box isn't just a container — it's routine, memory, and someone's idea of care. But cravings don't stay put. Office lunches get rushed, tastes wander, and everyone's secretly eyeing the tray next to them. CRAVE isn't the opposite of a lunch box — it's what it grows into. One insight, four formats: nostalgia, office humour, emotion, and a meme or two nobody asked for but everyone will forward.

Deliverable 01 — Instagram Carousel

Nobody Actually Wanted Their Own Lunch.

Hook: "Nobody actually wanted their own lunch."
What's the idea?
Every office runs on the same secret: nobody wants their own lunch. The carousel opens on that shared truth, then flips it — what if everyone could order exactly what they were craving, together, in one shot? That's where CRAVE walks in.
How does it connect?
The lunch box is routine. CRAVE is choice. It doesn't replace the ritual of eating together — it just lets everyone stop side-eyeing each other's tiffins.
Why does it capture attention?
It opens on something almost everyone has lived through, so people don't scroll past — they nod. By the time CRAVE shows up, it doesn't feel like an ad. It feels like the thing everyone already wanted.

Deliverable 02 — Instagram Static Post

There's Always Room for One More Craving.

Hook: "There's always room for one more craving."
What's the idea?
Every tiffin has that one compartment that always looks a little empty — like it was saving space for something else. That's the whole insight: there's always room for one more craving.
How does it connect?
The lunch box does the storytelling here. CRAVE is simply what fills the gap — not a replacement for home food, just its perfect plus-one.
Why does it capture attention?
One clean visual, one line, zero clutter. It asks the viewer to feel it before they read it — and that little pause is exactly what makes people stop scrolling.

Deliverable 03 — Instagram Static Post

What the Lunch Box Promised vs. What It Actually Delivered.

Hook: "The lunch box art always lied to us."

Every '90s kid remembers the lunch box art — burgers, pizza, fries, a donut for good measure — and then opening the lid to find aloo sabzi. This is that exact memory, drawn just how it felt.

Deliverable 06 — Instagram Reel

The Last First Bite.

Hook: "It was never just about the food."
What's the idea?
A story about someone who grew up on a lunch packed with love, every single day — and years later, away from home, realises it was never really about the food.
How does it connect?
The lunch box carries what a mother's cooking always meant — care, routine, home. CRAVE doesn't try to replace that. It just helps recreate the feeling of being looked after, wherever home is.
Why does it capture attention?
Most food-delivery content sells speed. This one sells a feeling first, and only brings in the brand once the emotion has already landed — which is exactly why it stays with you.

Campaign Conclusion

One lunch box. Six formats. One idea that never needed a seventh.

Carousel, static post, meme or reel — every format here comes from the same starting point: a lunch box was never just about food. It's memories, routines, and small everyday rituals. Humour gets the attention. Nostalgia gets the like. But it's the emotion that gets remembered — and that's the one thing every good campaign needs, whatever format it's wearing.

AI Usage (Part 3)

The concept, storytelling direction and campaign strategy are mine. AI came in during execution — I used Claude to pressure-test and sharpen the original ideas, then ChatGPT (prompted to act as a creative director) to turn them into production-ready briefs. Those prompts went into ChatGPT Image Generation and Google Gemini for the carousel, posts, and memes. The reel was built in Google AI Studio (Veo) from scene-by-scene prompts, then edited together. AI moved things faster — the insight, direction, and final picks are mine.
